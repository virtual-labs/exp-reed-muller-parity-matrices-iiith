Reed-Muller Codes : Generator and Parity Check Matrices and the Minimum Distance

Generator Matrix Construction for RM Codes

While the polynomial evaluation method provides a powerful algebraic definition for Reed-Muller codes, an alternative and equally fundamental approach is to construct their generator matrix directly using linear algebra, specifically the Kronecker product. This method reveals the code's recursive structure and provides a direct path to its implementation.

1. The Kronecker Product

The Kronecker product, denoted by the symbol ⊗\otimes⊗, is an operation on two matrices of arbitrary size, resulting in a larger block matrix.

If AAA is an m×nm \times nm×n matrix and BBB is a p×qp \times qp×q matrix, their Kronecker product A⊗BA \otimes BA⊗B is the mp×nqmp \times nqmp×nq block matrix:

A⊗B=(a11Ba12B⋯a1nBa21Ba22B⋯a2nB⋮⋮⋱⋮am1Bam2B⋯amnB)(1) A \otimes B = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11}B & a_{12}B & \cdots & a_{1n}B \\ a_{21}B & a_{22}B & \cdots & a_{2n}B \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{m1}B & a_{m2}B & \cdots & a_{mn}B \end{pmatrix} \tag{1} A⊗B=​a11​Ba21​B⋮am1​B​a12​Ba22​B⋮am2​B​⋯⋯⋱⋯​a1n​Ba2n​B⋮amn​B​​(1)

Essentially, each element aija_{ij}aij​ of matrix AAA is replaced by the scaled matrix aijBa_{ij}Baij​B.

The Kronecker product is associative, meaning we can extend it to multiple matrices in a straightforward sequence:

A⊗B⊗C=(A⊗B)⊗C=A⊗(B⊗C)(2) A \otimes B \otimes C = (A \otimes B) \otimes C = A \otimes (B \otimes C) \tag{2} A⊗B⊗C=(A⊗B)⊗C=A⊗(B⊗C)(2)

This allows us to define the mmm-fold Kronecker product of a matrix with itself, denoted G⊗mG^{\otimes m}G⊗m.

2. The RM Generator Kernel

The entire family of Reed-Muller codes can be constructed from the mmm-fold Kronecker product of a simple 2×22 \times 22×2 kernel matrix, operating in the binary field F2\mathbb{F}_2F2​:

G2=(1101)(3) G_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \tag{3} G2​=(10​11​)(3)

The matrix for an RM(m,m)RM(m, m)RM(m,m) code, which is the space of all possible Boolean functions in mmm variables, is given by G2⊗mG_2^{\otimes m}G2⊗m​. This is a 2m×2m2^m \times 2^m2m×2m matrix.

3. Selecting Rows for the Generator Matrix

The generator matrix for a specific RM(r,m)RM(r,m)RM(r,m) code is a submatrix of G2⊗mG_2^{\otimes m}G2⊗m​, formed by selecting a specific subset of its rows.

An observation on the matrix G2⊗mG_2^{\otimes m}G2⊗m​ is that the Hamming weight of its rows takes values 2k2^k2k for k∈{0,1,...,m}k \in \{0, 1, ..., m\}k∈{0,1,...,m}. The selection rule is based on these weights:

The generator matrix for the RM(r,m)RM(r,m)RM(r,m) code, denoted GRM(r,m)G_{RM(r,m)}GRM(r,m)​, is formed by taking all rows of G2⊗mG_2^{\otimes m}G2⊗m​ whose Hamming weight is greater than or equal to 2m−r2^{m-r}2m−r.

  • Number of Rows: The number of rows selected by this rule is precisely k=∑i=0r(mi)k = \sum_{i=0}^r \binom{m}{i}k=∑i=0r​(im​), which is the dimension of the RM(r,m)RM(r,m)RM(r,m) code.
  • Linear Independence: Since G2G_2G2​ is an invertible matrix, its mmm-fold Kronecker product G2⊗mG_2^{\otimes m}G2⊗m​ is also invertible. This means all of its 2m2^m2m rows are linearly independent. Therefore, any subset of these rows—including the set we select for our generator matrix—is also linearly independent.

4. Connection to the Polynomial Definition

This constructive method is perfectly equivalent to the polynomial evaluation definition. The rows of G2⊗mG_2^{\otimes m}G2⊗m​ are, in fact, the evaluation vectors of all possible monomials of mmm variables.

The correspondence is as follows: Let the binary representation of the row index jjj be (b1b2…bm)(b_1 b_2 \dots b_m)(b1​b2​…bm​), where b1b_1b1​ is the most significant bit (MSB). The jjj-th row of G2⊗mG_2^{\otimes m}G2⊗m​ corresponds to the evaluation vector of the monomial Mj=∏i=1mXibiM_j = \prod_{i=1}^m X_i^{b_i}Mj​=∏i=1m​Xibi​​. For instance, if m=3m=3m=3 and j=5j=5j=5, its binary representation is (101)2(101)_2(101)2​, so the monomial is X11X20X31=X1X3X_1^1 X_2^0 X_3^1 = X_1 X_3X11​X20​X31​=X1​X3​.

The Hamming weight of the evaluation vector for a monomial of degree ddd is exactly 2m−d2^{m-d}2m−d.

Therefore, our row selection rule, weight ≥2m−r\ge 2^{m-r}≥2m−r, can be translated into the polynomial degree:

weight(Eval(Mj))≥2m−r  ⟹  2m−d≥2m−r  ⟹  m−d≥m−r  ⟹  d≤r(11) \text{weight}( \text{Eval}(M_j) ) \ge 2^{m-r} \implies 2^{m-d} \ge 2^{m-r} \implies m-d \ge m-r \implies d \le r \tag{11} weight(Eval(Mj​))≥2m−r⟹2m−d≥2m−r⟹m−d≥m−r⟹d≤r(11)

This shows that selecting rows with weight at least 2m−r2^{m-r}2m−r is identical to selecting the basis functions (monomials) of degree at most rrr.

5. The Duality of Reed-Muller Codes

A remarkable property of RM codes is that they are duals of each other. The dual of a linear code C\mathcal{C}C, denoted C⊥\mathcal{C}^{\perp}C⊥, is the set of all vectors that are orthogonal to every codeword in C\mathcal{C}C. A generator matrix for one code can serve as a parity-check matrix for its dual.

Theorem: The dual of the RM(r,m)RM(r, m)RM(r,m) code is the RM(m−r−1,m)RM(m-r-1, m)RM(m−r−1,m) code.

[RM(r,m)]⊥=RM(m−r−1,m)(12) [RM(r, m)]^{\perp} = RM(m-r-1, m) \tag{12} [RM(r,m)]⊥=RM(m−r−1,m)(12)

This implies that a generator matrix for RM(m−r−1,m)RM(m-r-1, m)RM(m−r−1,m) is a valid parity-check matrix for RM(r,m)RM(r, m)RM(r,m). Let GrG_rGr​ be the generator matrix for RM(r,m)RM(r,m)RM(r,m) and let H=Gm−r−1H = G_{m-r-1}H=Gm−r−1​ be the generator matrix for RM(m−r−1,m)RM(m-r-1,m)RM(m−r−1,m). To prove the duality, we must show that GrHT=0G_r H^T = \mathbf{0}Gr​HT=0.

Proof: This condition is satisfied if the dot product of any row from GrG_rGr​ with any row from HHH is zero. Let's analyze this using the polynomial representation.

  1. A row in GrG_rGr​ is the evaluation vector Eval(f)\text{Eval}(f)Eval(f) of a basis monomial f(X)f(\mathbf{X})f(X) where deg(f)≤r\text{deg}(f) \le rdeg(f)≤r.
  2. A row in HHH is the evaluation vector Eval(g)\text{Eval}(g)Eval(g) of a basis monomial g(X)g(\mathbf{X})g(X) where deg(g)≤m−r−1\text{deg}(g) \le m-r-1deg(g)≤m−r−1.

The dot product of these two vectors is the sum of their component-wise product:

Eval(f)⋅Eval(g)=∑v∈F2mf(v)g(v)=∑v∈F2m(f⋅g)(v)(13) \text{Eval}(f) \cdot \text{Eval}(g) = \sum_{\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{F}_2^m} f(\mathbf{v}) g(\mathbf{v}) = \sum_{\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{F}_2^m} (f \cdot g)(\mathbf{v}) \tag{13} Eval(f)⋅Eval(g)=v∈F2m​∑​f(v)g(v)=v∈F2m​∑​(f⋅g)(v)(13)

The degree of the product polynomial p(X)=f(X)g(X)p(\mathbf{X}) = f(\mathbf{X})g(\mathbf{X})p(X)=f(X)g(X) is:

deg(p)=deg(f)+deg(g)≤r+(m−r−1)=m−1(14) \text{deg}(p) = \text{deg}(f) + \text{deg}(g) \le r + (m-r-1) = m-1 \tag{14} deg(p)=deg(f)+deg(g)≤r+(m−r−1)=m−1(14)

We use a fundamental property of Boolean functions: for any non-zero Boolean polynomial p(X)p(\mathbf{X})p(X) with degree less than mmm, the sum of its evaluations over all 2m2^m2m points is zero.

∑v∈F2mp(v)=0(15) \sum_{\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{F}_2^m} p(\mathbf{v}) = 0 \tag{15} v∈F2m​∑​p(v)=0(15)

Since deg(p)<m\text{deg}(p) < mdeg(p)<m, the dot product is zero. This holds for any pair of basis vectors (rows), proving the duality.

6. Minimum Distance from the Generator Matrix Construction

The recursive construction of the generator matrix provides an elegant way to prove the minimum distance of an RM(r,m)RM(r,m)RM(r,m) code. We can prove by induction that the minimum Hamming distance is exactly dmin=2m−rd_{min} = 2^{m-r}dmin​=2m−r.

Theorem: The minimum distance of the RM(r,m)RM(r,m)RM(r,m) code is 2m−r2^{m-r}2m−r.

Proof: We will use induction on mmm.

Base Cases:

  1. For r=0r=0r=0, the RM(0,m)RM(0,m)RM(0,m) code is the repetition code with codewords 0=(0,…,0)\mathbf{0} = (0, \dots, 0)0=(0,…,0) and 1=(1,…,1)\mathbf{1} = (1, \dots, 1)1=(1,…,1). The minimum distance is the weight of the all-ones vector, which is 2m2^m2m. The formula gives dmin=2m−0=2md_{min} = 2^{m-0} = 2^mdmin​=2m−0=2m. This holds.
  2. For r=mr=mr=m, the RM(m,m)RM(m,m)RM(m,m) code is the entire space F22m\mathbb{F}_2^{2^m}F22m​. The minimum distance is 1 (e.g., the codeword (0,…,0,1)(0, \dots, 0, 1)(0,…,0,1)). The formula gives dmin=2m−m=20=1d_{min} = 2^{m-m} = 2^0 = 1dmin​=2m−m=20=1. This also holds.

Inductive Hypothesis: Assume that for all r′≤m′r' \le m'r′≤m′, where m′<mm' < mm′<m, the minimum distance of the RM(r′,m′)RM(r', m')RM(r′,m′) code is 2m′−r′2^{m'-r'}2m′−r′.

Inductive Step: To construct the generator matrix for RM(r,m)RM(r,m)RM(r,m), we select rows from G2⊗mG_2^{\otimes m}G2⊗m​. Recall the recursive structure:

G2⊗m=(G2⊗(m−1)G2⊗(m−1)0G2⊗(m−1))(19) G_2^{\otimes m} = \begin{pmatrix} G_2^{\otimes (m-1)} & G_2^{\otimes (m-1)} \\ \mathbf{0} & G_2^{\otimes (m-1)} \end{pmatrix} \tag{19} G2⊗m​=(G2⊗(m−1)​0​G2⊗(m−1)​G2⊗(m−1)​​)(19)

The rows of GRM(r,m)G_{RM(r,m)}GRM(r,m)​ can be partitioned into two sets based on this structure:

  1. Rows from the top block: These correspond to basis monomials of degree at most rrr using only the first m−1m-1m−1 variables (X1,…,Xm−1X_1, \dots, X_{m-1}X1​,…,Xm−1​). These are precisely the basis vectors for the RM(r,m−1)RM(r, m-1)RM(r,m−1) code. In the mmm-variable space, these rows have the form (v∣v)(\mathbf{v} | \mathbf{v})(v∣v), where v\mathbf{v}v is a basis vector of RM(r,m−1)RM(r, m-1)RM(r,m−1). The generator matrix for this part is thus (GRM(r,m−1)∣GRM(r,m−1))(G_{RM(r, m-1)} | G_{RM(r, m-1)})(GRM(r,m−1)​∣GRM(r,m−1)​).

  2. Rows from the bottom block: These correspond to basis monomials that include the variable XmX_mXm​. They have the form Xm⋅M(X1,…,Xm−1)X_m \cdot M(X_1, \dots, X_{m-1})Xm​⋅M(X1​,…,Xm−1​), where the degree of MMM is at most r−1r-1r−1. The vectors generated by these monomials are the basis vectors for the RM(r−1,m−1)RM(r-1, m-1)RM(r−1,m−1) code, but shifted into the second half of the codeword. These rows have the form (0∣u)(\mathbf{0} | \mathbf{u})(0∣u), where u\mathbf{u}u is a basis vector of RM(r−1,m−1)RM(r-1, m-1)RM(r−1,m−1). The generator matrix for this part is (0∣GRM(r−1,m−1))(\mathbf{0} | G_{RM(r-1, m-1)})(0∣GRM(r−1,m−1)​).

Combining these, the generator matrix for RM(r,m)RM(r,m)RM(r,m) has the following block structure:

GRM(r,m)=(GRM(r,m−1)GRM(r,m−1)0GRM(r−1,m−1))(20) G_{RM(r,m)} = \begin{pmatrix} G_{RM(r, m-1)} & G_{RM(r, m-1)} \\ \mathbf{0} & G_{RM(r-1, m-1)} \end{pmatrix} \tag{20} GRM(r,m)​=(GRM(r,m−1)​0​GRM(r,m−1)​GRM(r−1,m−1)​​)(20)

Now consider an arbitrary non-zero codeword c∈RM(r,m)\mathbf{c} \in RM(r,m)c∈RM(r,m). It is generated by a message vector, which we can partition as (a∣b)(\mathbf{a} | \mathbf{b})(a∣b), where a\mathbf{a}a is the part for the top block and b\mathbf{b}b is for the bottom.

c=(a∣b)⋅GRM(r,m)=(aGRM(r,m−1)∣aGRM(r,m−1))+(0∣bGRM(r−1,m−1))(21) \mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{a} | \mathbf{b}) \cdot G_{RM(r,m)} = (\mathbf{a} G_{RM(r, m-1)} | \mathbf{a} G_{RM(r, m-1)}) + (\mathbf{0} | \mathbf{b} G_{RM(r-1, m-1)}) \tag{21} c=(a∣b)⋅GRM(r,m)​=(aGRM(r,m−1)​∣aGRM(r,m−1)​)+(0∣bGRM(r−1,m−1)​)(21)

Let u=a⋅GRM(r,m−1)\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{a} \cdot G_{RM(r, m-1)}u=a⋅GRM(r,m−1)​, which is a codeword in RM(r,m−1)RM(r, m-1)RM(r,m−1), and let v=b⋅GRM(r−1,m−1)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{b} \cdot G_{RM(r-1, m-1)}v=b⋅GRM(r−1,m−1)​, a codeword in RM(r−1,m−1)RM(r-1, m-1)RM(r−1,m−1). c=(u∣u+v)(22) \mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{u} | \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) \tag{22} c=(u∣u+v)(22)

This is the famous Plotkin construction, or (u∣u+v)(u|u+v)(u∣u+v) construction. The Hamming weight of c\mathbf{c}c is wt(c)=wt(u)+wt(u+v)wt(\mathbf{c}) = wt(\mathbf{u}) + wt(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})wt(c)=wt(u)+wt(u+v). We need to find the minimum possible weight for a non-zero c\mathbf{c}c.

We analyze two cases for the message (a∣b)(\mathbf{a} | \mathbf{b})(a∣b):

  • Case 1: b=0\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{0}b=0 and a≠0\mathbf{a} \neq \mathbf{0}a=0 In this case, v=0\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}v=0 and u\mathbf{u}u is a non-zero codeword in RM(r,m−1)RM(r, m-1)RM(r,m−1). The codeword is c=(u∣u)\mathbf{c} = (\mathbf{u} | \mathbf{u})c=(u∣u). The weight is wt(c)=2⋅wt(u)wt(\mathbf{c}) = 2 \cdot wt(\mathbf{u})wt(c)=2⋅wt(u). The minimum weight in this case is twice the minimum distance of RM(r,m−1)RM(r, m-1)RM(r,m−1). By our inductive hypothesis, this is:

    wtmin,1=2⋅dmin(RM(r,m−1))=2⋅(2(m−1)−r)=2m−r(23) wt_{min, 1} = 2 \cdot d_{min}(RM(r, m-1)) = 2 \cdot (2^{(m-1)-r}) = 2^{m-r} \tag{23} wtmin,1​=2⋅dmin​(RM(r,m−1))=2⋅(2(m−1)−r)=2m−r(23)

  • Case 2: b≠0\mathbf{b} \neq \mathbf{0}b=0 In this case, v\mathbf{v}v is a non-zero codeword in RM(r−1,m−1)RM(r-1, m-1)RM(r−1,m−1). Since v\mathbf{v}v and u+v\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}u+v are vectors in a linear code, their sum v+(u+v)=u\mathbf{v}+(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{u}v+(u+v)=u is also in a linear space. The weight of the codeword is wt(c)=wt(u)+wt(u+v)wt(\mathbf{c}) = wt(\mathbf{u}) + wt(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})wt(c)=wt(u)+wt(u+v). It is known from the properties of the (u∣u+v)(u|u+v)(u∣u+v) construction that the weight of any such codeword is at least the minimum distance of the code that produces the v\mathbf{v}v part. More directly, the minimum weight of v\mathbf{v}v is the minimum distance of RM(r−1,m−1)RM(r-1, m-1)RM(r−1,m−1). By our inductive hypothesis: wt(v)≥dmin(RM(r−1,m−1))=2(m−1)−(r−1)=2m−r(24) wt(\mathbf{v}) \ge d_{min}(RM(r-1, m-1)) = 2^{(m-1)-(r-1)} = 2^{m-r} \tag{24} wt(v)≥dmin​(RM(r−1,m−1))=2(m−1)−(r−1)=2m−r(24) Since the second half of the codeword c\mathbf{c}c is u+v\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}u+v, its weight is wt(u+v)wt(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v})wt(u+v). The total weight is wt(c)=wt(u)+wt(u+v)wt(\mathbf{c}) = wt(\mathbf{u}) + wt(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v})wt(c)=wt(u)+wt(u+v). The codewords form a linear code, so the minimum distance is the minimum weight of any non-zero codeword. For any codeword where v≠0\mathbf{v} \ne 0v=0, its weight is at least dmin(RM(r−1,m−1))=2m−rd_{min}(RM(r-1, m-1)) = 2^{m-r}dmin​(RM(r−1,m−1))=2m−r.

Combining both cases, the minimum weight of any non-zero codeword is the minimum of the minimums from each case:

dmin(RM(r,m))=min⁡(2m−r,2m−r)=2m−r(25) d_{min}(RM(r,m)) = \min(2^{m-r}, 2^{m-r}) = 2^{m-r} \tag{25} dmin​(RM(r,m))=min(2m−r,2m−r)=2m−r(25)

The induction holds, and the proof is complete.
